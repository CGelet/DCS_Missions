Previously

OP DARK HORSE2 / 19
The story so far

With the radar gone, the launchers lost their warning and lost one of their number on the first night. The brigade adapted: now they only move in bad weather, when nothing can see them from above. Today the weather is terrible, and three of them have moved.

OP DARK HORSE3 / 19
Why it matters

But the Georgian army has had a team in those hills for a week. They can see what the jets can't, and they have a GPS. The plan is simple and strange: launch with bombs and no target, hold above the cloud, and wait for a voice from the forest to tell you where to drop them.

Solid overcast from 3,000 to 9,000 ft with rain under it over the hills north of Ochamchire. A Scud launcher has moved to a hide on one of the hill roads. The plan

How it plays out

OP DARK HORSE12 / 19
Phase 1 · 13:10

Launch

Cold start at Batumi in the rain. Climb straight through the cloud on instruments to 20,000 ft: tops are 9,000 ft. Set up the JDAMs for pre-planned targets with the coordinates blank.

OP DARK HORSE13 / 19
Phase 2 · 13:38

Hold on Top

Hold at 22,000 ft over HOLD, above the weather. SHADOW will pass the launcher's coordinates as degrees, minutes and decimal minutes. Write them down, read them back, and enter them in the JDAMs. Elevation comes too.

OP DARK HORSE14 / 19
Phase 3 · 13:45

Release

Run in toward the coordinates and release inside the JDAM's range from 22,000 ft. You won't see the target or the impacts. SHADOW will tell you what happened.

OP DARK HORSE15 / 19
Phase 4 · 13:55

Home

South above the weather and an ILS to Batumi runway 13. It's raining there too.
