Why we're flying this

The day traps went well enough that the strike group gave the detachment the deck again, after dark. Night quals are the hardest landings in naval aviation: no horizon, a small lit rectangle moving at 15 knots, and the knowledge that the only alternate is 60 km away.

You start on the deck tonight. Launch, go to marshal like it's the end of a real mission, and come home on the numbers. PADDLES grades every pass. WREN will be on the platform again.

Briefing

Event overview

Night, broken cloud at 3,500 ft hiding a bright moon: dark below the layer. You start cold on the Stennis, 35 km west of Batumi, steaming east into a 10 kt land breeze at 15 kt (BRC 080). Launch, depart to SPLIT, then fly to MARSHAL, 21 nm astern on the 260 radial at 6,000 ft. Push, and fly the Case III approach to two traps with a launch between them. TEXACO orbits overhead at 6,000 ft.

Learning objectives

  • primary
    Two night arrested landings aboard the Stennis.
  • secondary
    Reach the 10-mile gate within ±30 s of plan after pushing from MARSHAL.
  • tertiary
    Fuel above bingo at all times: tank on TEXACO if you need to.

Range and weapons clearance

No weapons. Master arm SAFE. Every instruction from Marshal and PADDLES is mandatory.

Training rules

  1. Case III. Marshal on the 260 radial at 21 nm, angels 6. Push from MARSHAL on your own: the sim times you to the 10-mile gate.
  2. Platform at 5,000 ft: slow to 250 kt and 2,000 ft/min. At 10 nm, 1,200 ft; dirty up at 8 nm.
  3. Two arrested landings. A trap counts only after you've climbed above 1,000 ft since the last one.
  4. Bolter: climb straight ahead to 1,200 ft, then the bolter pattern back to the 6 nm point.
  5. Bingo 4,500 lb to Kobuleti (TACAN 67X). TEXACO is overhead at 6,000 ft: tank below 6,000 lb.

Knock-it-off and terminate

Vertigo, a bad ICLS, or anything that doesn't feel right on a dark night: call "Knock it off", climb straight ahead to 2,000 ft, level the wings on the instruments and tell Mother. You'll be sent back to marshal. Nobody gets graded for pressing on.

Special subjects

  • The black hole: under the layer there's no horizon tonight. The boat's lights are the only thing outside.
  • ICLS needles versus the ball: fly the needles until the ball is in sight, then the ball.
  • Night tanking on the S-3B: lights, slow closure, and no stabbing at the basket.
  • Fatigue after a bolter: the second pass is where people get low. Fly the numbers again.
