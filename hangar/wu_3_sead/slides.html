Why we're flying this

The Georgian Air Defence Command keeps a captured SA-6 battery and an SA-8 on the steppe east of the Gareja range for exactly this: letting allied crews hear a real Soviet-era radar on the RWR and shoot it. Today they've agreed to let the detachment put two live HARMs into the old radars. The launchers stay; the radars were due to be scrapped anyway.

TRAINING LOUD NEIGHBOURS3 / 23
Where it fits

You'll get one pass at each emitter, on a timeline. The radars will lock you the whole time. They won't fire. What's graded is whether you shoot on time, at the right thing, and turn away like it was real.

Event overview

The training area

Single ship from Vaziani to the SPARTAN emitter site, 50 km east, on the steppe 12 km northeast of David Gareja. The SA-6 radar and three launchers sit in a revetted battery position; the SA-8 is 4 km northwest of it. Both radiate and lock but are on weapons hold. Check in at SPARTAN IP, then fly to SHOT 1 and SHOT 2 on the timeline, firing one HARM at each cleared radar. The Azerbaijan border is 10 km east of the site.

TRAINING LOUD NEIGHBOURS10 / 23
Training rules

How we fight safe

  1. The range emitters are live radars on weapons hold. They will lock you; they will not fire. Treat every lock as real in your tactics.
  2. Cleared targets: the SA-6 search-and-track radar and the SA-8. The SA-6 launchers are not cleared: hitting one is a range foul.
  3. Shot 1 (SA-6 radar) from SHOT 1, Shot 2 (SA-8) from SHOT 2. Each point has a ±20 s window from the IP.
  4. Altitude block 20,000 to 26,000 ft until both shots are off. Nothing below 5,000 ft AGL on the range at any time.
  5. Range boundary: the Azerbaijan border, 10 km east of the site. Turn away from it after every shot.
  6. Bingo 4,000 lb to Vaziani.

Knock-it-off / terminate: Anyone can call "Knock it off" for a border infringement, an altitude bust or a hung missile. Turn west, climb to 26,000 ft, master arm SAFE, and wait for SPARTAN to call the range hot again.

TRAINING LOUD NEIGHBOURS11 / 23
Special subjects

Talk it through

  1. Range-known versus self-protect HARM modes: why we brief a pre-planned shot and still carry the TOO page.
  2. What the RWR shows when the SA-6 goes from search to track. Hear it, call it, then shoot.
  3. HARM time of flight from 40 km: about 75 s. What do you do with that time?
  4. The SA-8 on its own: it's short range and mobile. Don't fly inside 10 km of it to make the shot.
