The story so far

With the SA-11 gone, the column did what everyone expected: it came south. By last night its lead battalion was through Tskhinvali and dug in at Ergneti, five kilometres from Gori. The Georgian 4th Brigade holds the northern edge of the town with infantry, a company of T-72s and not much else.

At 0900 the brigade's radios started picking up the attack orders. They know it's coming this morning. They don't know which way. HAMMER, the brigade's JTAC, will be on the radio when you check in.

Briefing

Situation

The Georgian 4th Brigade holds the northern edge of Gori. A mechanized battalion is dug in at Ergneti, 5 km north, and its attack is expected this morning. Two approaches are possible: south down the highway from Ergneti, or across country from the northeast past Tirdznisi. The Georgians have T-72s too, in hull-down positions along their line. HAMMER will call the attack's axis and pass 9-lines. Expect mobile short-range air defence with the attack.

Objectives

  • primary
    Stop the attack before it reaches the Georgian line.
  • secondary
    No fratricide: every pass on HAMMER's clearance, positively identified.
  • tertiary
    Recover both aircraft to Vaziani.

Rules of engagement

Release only on HAMMER's clearance. Weapons free on vehicles moving south of Ergneti toward Gori. Nothing within 500 m of the Georgian line without HAMMER confirming. Weapons tight in Gori itself.
